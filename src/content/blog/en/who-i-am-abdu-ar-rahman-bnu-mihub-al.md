<p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]" dir="rtl" style="text-align: right;"><span style="font-family: Amiri;">بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم،&nbsp;</span></p><p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]" dir="rtl" style="text-align: right;"><span style="font-family: Amiri;">الحمد لله والصلاة والسلام على رسول الله</span></p>
<p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]">I am a Salafī seeker of knowledge, following the methodology of the Salaf as-Ṣāliḥ in understanding the Qurʾān and Sunnah — free from blind following of any school, and free from the leniency of those who dilute the texts to suit the times.</p>
<p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]">My path began independently. I was drawn early to the works of Shaykh al-Albānī, whose approach grounded my thinking. I studied books like Ibn Ḥazm's <em>al-Muḥallā, </em>and especially this one,&nbsp;for its disciplined argumentation and rigorous use of evidence. Over time, I came to understand that sound knowledge requires not only reading texts, but understanding how the Salaf themselves understood and lived them — so I invested heavily in that. I especially read Tāreekh al-Islām more than other books, and it taught me how oblivious I am to how the Salaf actually thought, and how far we are from that.</p>
<p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]">My research led me to the scholars of Yemen from Ahlu as-Sunnah, whose methodology I found to be consistent with what I had already learned and trusted. I consult them (not as much as I would like, due to the distance), benefit from their fatwās. Shaykh Abū al-Yamān ʿAdnān al-Maṣqarī — may Allah preserve him — has written a preface for one of my works, which stands as a testimony to the approach I follow.</p>
<p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]">I am largely self-taught, and I say this openly. What matters is not the form of one's learning, but its soundness — verified always against the Book, the Sunnah, and the understanding of the Salaf.</p>
<p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]">Anyone who does not feel comfortable taking knowledge from me is free not to. That is their right, and I respect it.</p>
