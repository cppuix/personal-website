<span style="font-family: IBM Plex Sans Arabic;">Many people today claim that photography is nothing but reflection, 
and that what appears in a photo is just like what is seen in a mirror, 
and therefore harmless. This is astonishing, and it is one of the most 
corrupt analogies, because the differences between photographs and 
mirrors are many.</span>
<br /><div class="telegram-text"><p><span style="font-family: IBM Plex Sans Arabic;">To clarify, let us consider some basic scientific facts.</span></p>
<h3 style="text-align: left;"><span style="font-family: IBM Plex Sans Arabic;">First: The Definition of Reflection</span></h3><p><span style="font-family: IBM Plex Sans Arabic;">Reflection is when light bounces off a reflective surface, such as a 
mirror or calm water, allowing the object itself to be seen directly on 
that surface.</span></p>
<h3 style="text-align: left;"><span style="font-family: IBM Plex Sans Arabic;">Second: Conditions for Reflection</span></h3><p><span style="font-family: IBM Plex Sans Arabic;">For reflection to exist, two things are required:</span></p>
<ul><li><span style="font-family: IBM Plex Sans Arabic;">The object itself.</span></li><li><span style="font-family: IBM Plex Sans Arabic;">A reflective surface.</span></li></ul>
<p><span style="font-family: IBM Plex Sans Arabic;">And for the reflection to be visible, a direct line of sight between the eye and the reflective surface is necessary.</span></p>
<h3 style="text-align: left;"><span style="font-family: IBM Plex Sans Arabic;">Third: Why Photographs Are Not Reflections</span></h3><p><span style="font-family: IBM Plex Sans Arabic;">Photographs exist independently without fulfilling any of these conditions — a fact so obvious.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-family: IBM Plex Sans Arabic;">For example: suppose you liked a flower outside the house, 
photographed it with your phone, then went home and showed your brother 
the picture. Where is the flower? Where is the reflective surface? Where
 is the direct line between your brother’s eye and the reflective 
surface? All the conditions of reflection are absent, yet he still sees 
the picture.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-family: IBM Plex Sans Arabic;">Why? Because you copied the flower’s image with your phone camera. 
Instead of needing a canvas, paints, and a brush, you used a modern 
device to reproduce its image.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-family: IBM Plex Sans Arabic;">This is obvious, simple, and clear — and should not require such lengthy explanation.</span></p>
