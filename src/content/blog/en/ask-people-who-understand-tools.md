<p>Ask tech-savvy people, and do that early, not casually or as an afterthought. Early.</p><p>People who live around tools start seeing things others don't even realize exist. Little cracks, hidden hinges, quiet risks sitting behind ordinary buttons.</p><p>Most people think using a tool equals understanding it, but that’s rarely true.</p><p>You can post, upload, share links, create channels, grow followers, and still not understand the structure underneath any of it.</p><p>We know that the internet is structure, layers upon layers, systems talking to systems, permissions, identities, recovery methods, hidden doors you don't see until someone else walks through them first.</p><p>The internet requires expertise.</p><p>Not genius, or elite knowledge, just people who actually know what they're doing.</p><p>That kind of person is called: A specialist.<br />Not a guesser, and definitely not an improviser.</p><b>Before Competing, Understand the Tools</b><p>Many people speak about competing with falsehood online, spreading benefit, countering harm, being present where others are present.</p><p>That part makes sense.</p><p>But there is a skipped step.</p><p><b>Understanding the battlefield.</b></p><p>Because when people don't understand the tools they're using, they don't really control their efforts. They borrow systems without understanding their limits.</p><p>And when something goes wrong, they don't know how to respond properly, because they were never trained for it.</p><b>Good Intentions Need Technical Support</b><p>A person can be sincere, dedicated, motivated to benefit others.</p><p>But sincerity does not automatically produce technical awareness.</p><p>Just like someone can want to drive safely but still not understand how brakes fail, how tires wear down, how engines overheat.</p><p>You don't learn those things by intention.</p><p>You learn them from people who already know.</p><p>You need someone who has handled the tool long enough to know its moods.</p><p>Every field has them.</p><p>Photography has them.<br />Software has them.<br />Platforms have them.<br />Security has them.</p><p>And ignoring them doesn't make the risk disappear. It only makes the surprise bigger when something eventually breaks.</p><b>We Already Accept This in Other Fields</b><p>Think about photography.</p><p>I keep telling people: ask experts about photography before forming strong conclusions. Not because photography is mysterious, but because the technical side of it is deeper than it looks.</p><p>The same applies to platforms.</p><p>The same applies to the internet.</p><p>We shouldn't assume that because something looks simple on the surface, it is simple underneath.</p><p>Most of the time, simplicity is an interface — not a reality.</p><p>Behind every clean button sits a complicated mechanism.&nbsp;</p><b>The Internet Rewards Preparedness</b><p>The internet moves fast.</p><p>Mistakes spread faster than corrections. Confusion spreads faster than clarity. Problems multiply quietly before they become visible.</p><p>And once something goes wrong, it often affects more than the person running the platform.</p><p>It affects everyone connected to them: followers, readers, students, listeners.</p><p>That is why relying on expertise is a <b>responsibility</b>.</p><p>Not panic or suspicion, just responsibility. Careful and slow and steady.</p><hr /><p><b>Ask Before You Build</b></p><p>Before launching platforms.<br />Before building audiences.<br />Before depending on systems.</p><p>Ask the people who understand the tools.</p><p>And not just once, no: <b>Repeatedly</b>.</p><p>Let them point out risks you didn't see. Weaknesses you didn't imagine. Simple protections that cost little but prevent massive problems later.</p><p>Because the internet is not forgiving to assumption.</p><p>But it is surprisingly kind to preparation.</p><p>And many problems that look sudden… were preventable long before they happened.</p><p>If someone had just asked:</p><p>"Who here actually understands how this works?".</p><p>Finally, please don't assume these experts will know everything either, don't jump from one end to the other. They are humans who make mistakes too.</p><p>The Prophet ṣallā-Allāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam said:</p><p></p><blockquote>Whoever undertakes medical treatment among people without being known for medical practice beforehand, and he causes harm, is held responsible (liable for the damage).</blockquote>Source: Sunan Abī Dāwūd 4587.<br />Classed as ḥasan (sound) by al-Albānī in Ṣaḥīḥ Abī Dāwūd.<p></p><p>The point of evidence here is what we derive through mafhūm al-mukhālafah (argument by contrast), meaning that anyone who is known to possess the skill (profession) bears no liability.</p><p dir="rtl" style="text-align: right;">هذا والله أعلم.&nbsp;</p>
