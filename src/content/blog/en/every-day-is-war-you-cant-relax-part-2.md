More devils means more effort?<p>Or more wisdom?</p><p>"If there are many devils… then you must fight harder".</p><p>Sounds logical.</p><p>But is it?</p><p>If there are more enemies than you… what does constant fighting produce?</p><p>Victory?</p><p>No.</p><p>Collapse.</p><p>If twenty devils speak, and you try to shout louder than all twenty every day…you will lose your voice before they lose theirs…if they will, at all.</p><p>More activity is not always strength.</p><p>Sometimes it is wasted motion.</p><p>Like someone swinging a sword at shadows until his arms shake.</p><p>Fatigue is not bravery.</p>Allow to bring the words of al-Albānī again:<blockquote><p>We move along this path like a turtle — slow… painfully slow</p></blockquote><p>This line alone dismantles the fantasy of constant warfare.</p><p>Because the path of knowledge was never meant to be dramatic.</p><p>It was meant to be durable.</p><hr /><p>What does that alarmist language produce?</p><p>What does it quietly create inside people?</p><p>The answer is: Identity inflation.</p><p>"You are in a war every day, you can't relax".</p><p>That will be their constant state now, and that's the result of unmeasured speech.</p><p>No discipline…no knowledge…no patience, and now he is a "fighter".</p><p>Fighters like noise.</p><p>Students like silence.</p><p>Which one survives longer?</p><p>You already know the answer.</p><p>More devils in the world does not mean more frantic effort.</p><p>It means: More careful effort.</p><p>Less shouting, and more structuring.</p><p>Less reacting, and more building.</p><p>Less movement, and more direction.</p><p>Otherwise, you burn fuel… and go nowhere.</p><p>Like spinning wheels in mud.</p><p>Like a hamster in a wheel.&nbsp;</p><p>Lots of noise.</p><p>No distance.</p><hr /><h2>The Hidden Test</h2><p>Every idea should be tested by what it produces.</p><p>Ask: Does this produce endurance…or exhaustion?</p><p>If it produces exhaustion, it is probably theatrical strength.</p><p>Not real strength.</p><p>Theatrical strength cannot survive the long road.</p><hr /><h2>Summary</h2><p>The problem with the "war every day, no relaxing" mentality isn't just tone.</p><p>It's architecture.</p><ul><li><p>It turns life into permanent emergency mode.</p></li><li><p>It confuses activity with effectiveness.</p></li><li><p>It produces burnout instead of endurance.</p></li><li><p>It encourages noise instead of formation.</p></li><li><p>It replaces steady progress with dramatic exhaustion.</p></li><li><p>It ignores the model of slow, deliberate growth taught by the scholars.</p></li></ul><p>And the alternative isn't softness.</p><p>It is durability.</p><p>Go back to your normal momentum, relax, sip some coffee, drill those ideas and concepts, practice them, teach them.</p><p>Learn, understand, understand more, understand some more, read again, test, practice, understand more, realize you don't actually understand, learn more, get grounded.</p><p>This path is long, and drama has no place here.&nbsp;</p>
